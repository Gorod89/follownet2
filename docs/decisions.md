# Decisions and deviations from the specification

Every choice the specification leaves open and every deviation from it. Entries are
appended per milestone and never changed silently. `Deviation` marks entries that differ
from the text of the specification; the others fill a gap.

## Environment

| Id | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | One Python environment for the four projects of `research_plans`: `../cf-stability/.venv` (Python 3.11.8, PyTorch 2.11.0+cu128, numpy 2.4, pandas 3.0, pyarrow 25, scipy 1.17, hydra-core 1.3, osqp 1.1, pytest 9). `follownet2/.venv` holds no packages: two path files add the site-packages of that environment and the repository root. Only the cf-stability job installs packages there and announces version changes. On a clean machine: `pip install -e ".[dev]"`. | Decision of the user (2026-09-28); the volume had been full the day before and a copy of PyTorch with CUDA takes 4.8 GB. |
| E2 | No dataset was downloaded by this repository. Raw data are read in place, read-only, from `../cf-stability/data/raw` (NGSIM, OpenACC, three FollowNet event sets, Waymo pairs; every file has its URL and SHA-256 in `SOURCES.json` there). `scripts/download_data.py` exists and is tested offline; it downloads only with `--yes`. | Disk space, and downloads need the approval of the user. |
| E3 | Event directories are written in the eight-column layout shared with cf-stability. `storage.compact=true` leaves out the derived columns `s` and `dv`, which are recomputed bit for bit on reading; it was the default while the volume was full and remains available. | Lossless (`test_compact_layout_is_lossless_and_smaller`), 30 % smaller. |
| E4 | Every write of events checks the free space first and keeps a reserve (`storage.reserve_mb`). | Three other jobs write to the same volume; a full disk would break them. |
| E5 | The GPU is shared with three other jobs (95 % utilisation, 43 of 49 GB during milestone 1). Rollouts read nothing back from the device inside the time loop, the closed formulas and the integrator step are TorchScript functions (6.5 times faster under that load, identical results), and the calibration resumes after the last finished model when a run is started again (one `CUDA error: unknown error` in five runs). | Without these a dataset took more than an hour and one failure lost the run. |

## Interfaces

| Id | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Deviation.** The Event schema, the rollout, the stability module and the models are implemented in this repository instead of being imported from cf-stability, cf-identifiability and cf-uncertainty. Compatibility is kept at the interface: same fields, ids and directory layout as `cf_stability.data.schema` (a directory written by either side is read by the other, `test_wire_compatibility_with_cf_stability`), converters in `follownet2/compat.py`, and `wrap_model` for models with the three-channel history `(s, dv, v)`. | At the time of milestone 1 the three repositories are being written by parallel jobs: cf-stability had a schema and a data contract but no rollout, stability module or models, cf-uncertainty did not exist, none is an installable package. Importing from a moving target would make results irreproducible. To be revisited when the siblings reach their milestone 1. |
| S2 | The state history has four channels `(s, dv, v, a_prev)`. `a_prev[k] = a[k-1]` is the last acceleration known at `t[k]`. | The interface of the siblings carries `(s, dv, v)` only; the persistence baseline and autoregressive networks need the last acceleration, and a row must contain nothing from the future. |
| S3 | `a[k]` is the acceleration applied over `[t[k], t[k+1]]`. P0 asks for `a[k]` given the history up to `t[k]`; "predict a(t + 0.1)" of the specification is this quantity, whose effect is seen at `t + 0.1`. Persistence predicts `a[k-1]`, so its error is the jerk times 0.1 s by construction. | Makes the three P0 scores consistent with the integrator and the identity of E5 exact. |
| S4 | Warm-up: the first 30 samples (3 s) of every event are observed; all models are scored from sample 30 on, whatever their own history length (at most 30). The calibration rollouts start at the same sample. | Identical samples for all models. 3 s because reaction times of Gipps and Newell are calibrated up to 3 s. cf-stability rolls from sample 0. |
| S5 | P0 clips the predicted acceleration to `[-8, 4]` like every rollout (changed in milestone 2; milestone 1 scored the unclipped output). The persistence identity is exact where the recorded accelerations lie inside the range, which holds for the reconstructed version by construction; the share of recorded samples outside the range is reported with it. | Unclipped, the closed formulas are scored by a few states with a small gap: the one-step error of the IDM on NGSIM was 8.9 m/s^2, that of Newell 75 m/s^2. A prediction outside the range of the vehicle is a prediction of its limit. |
| S6 | Gipps and Newell are delay models. With the 0.1 s step they are evaluated at every step on the state delayed by the reaction time (linear interpolation between samples). Newell has no acceleration parameter; its acceleration is bounded by the clip of the rollout only. | The specification fixes the step, the models fix the delay. |

## Data

| Id | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Extraction criteria: one unchanged leader, no lane change of either vehicle, no change of the driving mode, at least 150 samples, `0.5 m <= s < 120 m`, follower moving (`v > 0.1 m/s`) in at least 80 % of the samples, optional cut of long runs. The constants are those of the data contract of cf-stability, including the smallest gap of 0.5 m it adopted on 2026-09-28, except for D2. | The FollowNet repository has no extraction code; the paper states the leader and duration rules and a standstill rule and no spacing limits. Net gaps below 0.5 m are overlapping or touching bounding boxes (886 samples of the Waymo pairs have `s <= 0`). |
| D2 | 15 s are 150 samples: a duration is counted as samples times step, as FollowNet does (its 15 s windows hold 375 samples at 25 Hz and 150 on the 10 Hz grid; 3 NGSIM and 2 Waymo events of FollowNet have exactly 150). `Event.duration`, first to last sample, is then 14.9 s. cf-stability requires 151 samples. | FollowNet compatibility is the requirement; with 151 samples none of the 12,541 highD events of FollowNet would pass. |
| D2a | Speeds between -0.5 m/s and 0 are set to zero and counted in `meta["negative_speeds_set_to_zero"]`; a speed below -0.5 m/s ends the event. | 7.3 % of the speed samples of the Waymo pairs (version 2) are negative, median -0.005 m/s, smallest -0.515 m/s: filter noise of standing vehicles. Treating them as invalid removed 44 % of the pairs. |
| D3 | The raw version keeps the acceleration of the source where it has one (NGSIM `v_acc`), otherwise the Savitzky-Golay derivative of the speed (window 1.1 s, order 2). | "Raw" means as published. |
| D4 | Reconstruction: constrained quadratic programme, see `docs/reconstruction.md`. It is not the method of Montanino and Punzo (2015) nor of Makridis and Kouvelas (2023). | The reconstructed NGSIM is no longer hosted; one method for all datasets; the result is exactly consistent with the integrator of the benchmark. |
| D5 | Regimes: density proxy `1000 / mean(s + L_lead)` veh/km per lane; `free` below 16.2, `congested` from 28.0, `steady` between (HCM level-of-service boundaries C/D and E/F). Labels come from the raw version and are shared by both versions. | The specification names the regimes, not the thresholds. |
| D6 | **Deviation.** Splits are folds: five folds, test 20 %, validation 15 %, training 65 % of the samples, instead of the 70/15/15 of FollowNet. The legacy event-level split uses the same proportions. | Paired comparison of split kinds needs equal proportions; folds give paired tests. |
| D7 | Validation parts are held out by follower in the driver, site and regime splits and by event in the event split. | Early stopping on leaked drivers would bias model selection. |
| D8 | The regime split leaves out of the development part every event whose follower also drives in the test part, unless this costs more than half of the development samples; it then stays overlapping and says so. | Otherwise the regime split inherits the driver leak. |
| D9 | The leakage check also reports, without failing, the share of test events whose leader is a follower of the training part. | The leader trajectory is an input, not a target; the overlap is a weaker dependence that the specification does not forbid. |
| D10 | Near-miss episodes are mined from the follower series with their own rules (`follownet2/data/rare_events.py`): triggers TTC < 2 s, DRAC > 3.35 m/s^2 (both only above 3 m/s and with a gap of at least 1 m), leader braking below -4 m/s^2, cut-in with a time gap below 1 s; window 5 s before and 10 s after the critical instant; the leader may change inside an episode. Episodes exist in the raw version only. | The 15 s rule of FollowNet removes most critical interactions. Without the speed and gap conditions 70 % of the candidates in the Waymo pairs were creeping queues (follower below 3 m/s) and 37 % had gaps below 2 m. |
| D11 | **Deviation.** Empirical near-miss episodes come from the datasets that are on disk (NGSIM, OpenACC, Waymo pairs, FollowNet sets), not from CitySim and TGSIM. The miner and the loaders of both sources exist; CitySim needs a signed request form, TGSIM a download of 1.5 GB that was not approved yet. | Access. |
| D12 | **Deviation.** `follownet_highd` (the highD events of FollowNet, CC0 on figshare) stands for highD in every experiment until raw highD is available. These events have no ids: every event is its own follower, so the driver-level split equals the event-level split on them and they cannot show a driver leak. | highD and exiD are given out by application (levelXdata); no session on this machine has them. |
| D13 | `follownet_waymo` is not used: 1,439 of its 1,440 events are whole pairs of Hu et al. (2022) with the centre-to-centre distance as spacing. `waymo_hu` holds the same pairs with net gaps and source ids. | Found by matching the speed series of both sources. The spacing of the FollowNet subsets is not defined uniformly: centre to centre (Waymo), highD `dhw` (HighD), front to front (NGSIM: the median spacing per speed class agrees within 0.7 m with the front-to-front distance of our own extraction of I-80, 4:00 to 4:15 pm, and exceeds our net gap by 3.9 to 5.2 m; the median leader length is 4.5 m). On these sets `s <= 0` is therefore not a collision and TTC is biased upwards; `meta["gap_definition"]` says which distance an event holds. |
| D14 | NGSIM: the 15-minute file of a row is the rank of `global_time - 100 * frame_id` within the location (three files per location, overlapping by 1.5 min). Vehicle ids are unique inside a file, `preceding` refers to the same file, the group of the loader is the file. | Verified on the raw table: 6,101 and 5,678 keys (file, vehicle id), none with a hole, all with `total_frames` rows. Rule communicated by the cf-stability session. |

## Models and calibration

| Id | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Objective `J = NRMSE(s) + NRMSE(v)`, `NRMSE(y) = RMSE(y) / RMS(y_obs)`, closed loop over the whole event after the warm-up. Collision: `J = 10 + share of samples with s <= 0`. Global parameters: pooled errors plus `10 * collision rate`. | Punzo, Zheng, Montanino (2021) recommend spacing-based measures in closed loop; the specification asks for spacing plus speed. Same objective as cf-stability. |
| C2 | Optimiser: differential evolution in torch, batched over events and candidates: best1bin, population `15 * D`, dithered mutation U(0.5, 1), recombination 0.7, Latin hypercube start, tolerance 0.01, at most 200 generations, population updated once per generation, no polishing. | SciPy defaults; SciPy is the reference in the tests. Per-event calibration of 1000 events takes 50 to 120 s per model on the GPU. |
| C3 | Bounds: IDM `v0 [10, 45]`, `T [0.3, 3]`, `s0 [0.5, 10]`, `a [0.2, 4]`, `b [0.5, 6]`; Gipps `a [0.5, 4]`, `b [0.5, 8]`, `V [10, 45]`, `tau [0.3, 3]`, `s0 [0.5, 10]`, `b_hat [0.5, 8]`; Newell `tau [0.3, 3]`, `d [0.5, 15]`, `V [10, 45]`. | Ranges used in the calibration literature; IDM bounds as in cf-stability. |
| C4 | The model scored by the benchmark is the globally calibrated one (training part of the split). Per-event calibration gives the parameter spread and the accuracy the model form can reach. | A per-event calibrated model has seen the test event. |
| C5 | Milestone 1 calibrates on samples: 1000 events per dataset and version for the per-event calibration, 2000 training events for the global one (`calibration.per_event_sample`, `calibration.global_sample`); the test parts are scored completely. | Time on the shared GPU: a dataset takes 6 to 60 minutes. |

## After the review of milestone 1 (2026-09-28)

The user left items R1 to R5 to the implementer, approved downloads and decided R6.

| Id | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Deviation.** H5.4 (rankings by spacing and by speed diverge) is tested on the closed-loop protocol P1. The Kendall tau of the P0 rankings is still reported, marked as degenerate. | In P0 the speed and spacing errors are the acceleration error times `dt` and `dt^2` on reconstructed data, and the inconsistency of the record on raw data (section 5 of `results/m1/REPORT.md`): the two rankings cannot differ for a reason that concerns the models. |
| R2 | **Deviation.** H5.2 (inflation by the event-level split) is tested with a paired design: the test events are the same for both training sets. For fold `k` of the driver split the events of every test driver are divided into probe events, which are scored, and leak events; the leaky training part is the clean one with a random subset replaced by the leak events, so both have the same size. All five folds, bootstrap over drivers. The unpaired comparison of the two splits is reported next to it. | On Waymo, where no driver has two events, the unpaired comparison differs by 6 to 9 % for IDM and 24 to 28 % for Newell without any leak. The design needs drivers with several events: OpenACC and 46 % of the NGSIM events. |
| R3 | A fifth split kind, `block`: every recording is cut into blocks of 300 s by the start time of the events, blocks are dealt to folds, and every development event that shares a vehicle, as follower or as leader, with the test part is left out. | 75 % (NGSIM) and 65 to 84 % (OpenACC) of the test events of the driver split are led by a vehicle that is a follower in the training part. |
| R4 | The calibration bounds stay (C3, equal to those of cf-stability). Every table flags parameters on a bound. The benchmark gets a second entry per mechanistic model, `<model>_median`: the median of the per-event estimates of the training part. | With the pooled objective `b` of IDM and `b`, `b_hat` of Gipps go to the lower bound on every dataset: a small `b` raises the gain on the relative speed, which lowers the pooled spacing error of heterogeneous drivers. Widening the bound would move the estimate further away from a driver. The median driver does not have this artefact and differs in string stability. |
| R5 | NGSIM is reconstructed with a cut-off of 0.35 Hz (other sources keep their values). All sources get an outlier step: weights of a Huber loss at 2.5 standard deviations, zero beyond 5, three reweightings. | Sample of 600 NGSIM events, `sigma_x = 0.5 m`: see the table in `docs/reconstruction.md`. At 0.35 Hz no event lies on the acceleration or jerk bound and the rms acceleration (0.76 m/s^2) equals that of the FollowNet events from the reconstruction of Montanino and Punzo (0.79); at 0.5 Hz 1.8 % of the events use the full braking bound. The outlier step changes little on NGSIM (0.05 % of the samples): the problem is moderate noise, not gross errors. |
| R6 | The events of `follownet_highd` are published with the benchmark (decision of the user), with the CC0 notice of figshare and the citation of FollowNet and highD. Events extracted from raw highD or exiD are still never published. | The FollowNet authors released these events under CC0. |
| R7 | TGSIM is downloaded (five freeway tables, 1.0 GB of 1.46 GB; Foggy Bottom, an urban arterial, is left out). UTE is not downloaded: it has no licence. | Approval of the user for downloads; TGSIM is public domain. |

## Milestone 2 (models)

| Id | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| M2-1 | The reference implementations are the benchmark's own, written from the papers against one interface (`docs/model_contract.md`); `docs/models.md` lists every difference from the papers and the constants that could not be checked. | The specification asks for reference implementations of seventeen models; none of the authors' codes fits the interface, several are unpublished. |
| M2-2 | Learned models read `(s, dv, v)` by default (`use_a_prev=False`); the last acceleration is an option. | As in the literature; with one second of speeds a network recovers the past acceleration anyway. |
| M2-3 | `mlp`, `gru`, `lstm` exist in two training variants: one-step (`mlp`, ...) and one-step plus a rollout term over 5 s (`mlp_loop`, ...). | One-step training is what most published one-step models do and it fails in closed loop (32 % collisions for the MLP on NGSIM); FollowNet's own baselines are trained in closed loop. Both belong in the benchmark. |
| M2-4 | Every model has `fit(train, val, cfg)`; supervised models use one trainer (AdamW, cosine schedule, early stopping on the one-step validation error, the state before training being a candidate); policy and meta learners bring their own loops; mechanistic models are calibrated by the protocol of milestone 1. | One training budget per family keeps the comparison fair; a hybrid that starts at its physics must not end worse than it. |
| M2-5 | A trained model is a checkpoint of plain tensors and constructor arguments (`torch.load(weights_only=True)`), with provenance (dataset, version, split, fold, seed, config hash, git revision). | Reproducibility; no code in files. |
| M2-6 | The sanity runs of milestone 2 use 4,000 training events, fold 0 of the driver split, seed 0, on `ngsim` and `follownet_highd` (reconstructed). They show that every model trains and how one-step and closed-loop scores relate; the experiments of milestone 4 use the full training parts, several seeds and folds. | Time on the shared GPU. |
| M2-7 | Adaptive models (`maml_lstm`) get the observed warm-up of every event once through `begin(hist)` and adapt a linear head per event; the protocols call the hook before the first prediction. | The interface `acc = model(history)` has no room for a per-event state; the hook keeps adaptation causal (observed rows only). |


## Milestone 3 (protocols)

The contract of the protocols is `docs/protocols.md`.

| Id | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| M3-1 | P1 horizons of 10 s and 30 s are full windows cut from the events with a stride equal to the horizon, every window rolled from the observed state at its start; the whole-event rollout stays as in milestone 2. Events shorter than warm-up plus horizon give no window of that horizon (the 15 s FollowNet events have no 30 s window). | Every simulated sample belongs to one window per horizon; the windows of all models are identical; the whole-event score keeps the long NGSIM events comparable with milestone 2. |
| M3-2 | P2 leader profiles: the ten AstaZero platoon runs of OpenACC (five vehicles, 13 to 28 m/s) and the ten ZalaZone dynamic-platform runs with the largest leader-speed variance (eleven vehicles, 8 to 17 m/s), each reduced to the 300 s window with the largest variance of the leader speed, plus one synthetic braking pulse (from the 95 % speed quantile of the training data, at most 25 m/s, to 40 % of it at -3 m/s^2, back at +1.5 m/s^2, then 240 s at the initial speed so that the wave, about 2 s per vehicle, reaches the hundredth follower). Every metric is reported over all profiles and over the profiles whose leader speeds lie inside the 1-99 % speed range of the training data; the composite score uses the latter. The recorded followers of the window (ACC vehicles in eight runs, human drivers in two) are the empirical reference of the concave-growth and hysteresis errors. One simulation of 100 followers per profile; the 20-vehicle platoon is its prefix. The platoon starts at the model's own equilibrium gap at the initial speed (two-vehicle settle of 120 s; with any other gap follower i must fall back by i times the difference, which no hold absorbs for 100 vehicles), then a 60 s hold. The wave speed is fitted over the first 20 followers so that models are comparable; the fit over all crossing vehicles is reported next to it. | The specification asks for at least five OpenACC profiles and an empirical curve; OpenACC is the only source with recorded platoons (five vehicles at AstaZero). A follower never depends on the vehicles behind it, so the prefix equals a separate 20-vehicle run. Half of the AstaZero windows start above 24 m/s, above the desired speed of the NGSIM-calibrated IDM (23.9 m/s) and outside the congested NGSIM range: on them the NGSIM models cannot even keep up with the leader, so their platoon metrics would measure the speed mismatch, not the model. |
| M3-3 | P3 numerical test: after a 120 s hold the leader steps by +0.05 and, in a second run, -0.05 m/s (linear regime); a second pass with steps of 1 m/s measures the finite-perturbation gain (`max_gain_large`, `unstable_fraction_large`, collisions after the step); a speed is an equilibrium only when the follower settles and returns to the leader's speed after the step (persistence and the constant-speed baseline have none); the impulse response of the follower speed (averaged over the two signs) gives the gain `G(f)` on 0.002-2 Hz by a discrete Fourier sum; unstable when `max G > 1 + 1e-5` (the mathematical criterion within the accuracy of float64 rollouts); the share with `max G > 1.001` (at least 0.1 % growth per vehicle) is reported next to it. | The peak gains of the calibrated IDMs lie between 1.000 and 1.09 at 0.005-0.06 Hz (`idm_partials`): a sinusoid sweep would need rollouts of 1000 s per frequency and speed to resolve them, the step response gives the whole band from one 300 s rollout. For a linear follower the two are the same transfer function. Near the analytic boundary the excess gain is only `m^2 / (8 f_s^2)`: a tolerance of 1e-3 would move the boundary of the IDM defaults from 18.6 to 16.8 m/s, 1e-5 keeps it at 18.3. A step of 0.2 m/s already leaves the linear regime of a network: the stability-regularised MLP of NGSIM has gains of 1.003 to 1.006 at 0.2 m/s and exactly 1 at 0.05 m/s, in line with its analytic margins, while at 1 m/s it is unstable at every speed (gains up to 1.11). |
| M3-4 | P4 synthetic suite: nine pulses (-4, -6, -8 m/s^2 from 15, 25, 35 m/s, by 10 m/s, back at +1 m/s^2), a full stop from 30 m/s at -4 m/s^2, four cut-ins (time gaps 0.5 and 1.0 s at 15 and 25 m/s) placed in front of the *simulated* follower. Empirical suite: the near-miss episodes mined on the raw tracks in milestone 1, reconstructed piecewise between leader changes and kept only when a trigger still holds on the reconstructed series; the TGSIM episodes are rolled by every model whatever its training dataset. An episode is kept only when a confirmed trigger fires after the 3 s warm-up (TGSIM: 4,473 of 5,326 episodes kept, 438 had their only trigger inside the warm-up; NGSIM: 3,524 of 9,348, the leader-braking trigger survives in 6 %). | A cut-in defined relative to the recorded follower would give every model a different time gap. The raw NGSIM and TGSIM kinematics make artefact triggers (milestone 1); CitySim is not available. |
| M3-5 | The composite score ranks the models on the concave-growth error, the hysteresis error, the numerical unstable fraction and the 100-vehicle collision count; its uncertainty comes from a bootstrap over the P2 profiles and the P3 speeds (and over training seeds in milestone 4). | P2 and P3 are simulations without a driver dimension: a bootstrap over drivers does not apply to them. |
| M3-6 | Protocol results are stored next to the checkpoint of the run (`results/models/.../<label>/seed<s>/<p>.json` and `<p>.<table>.parquet`) with the protocol configuration and its hash; `scripts/evaluate.py` recomputes only what changed. | One directory per trained model holds training, provenance and every protocol; runs are resumable on the shared machine. |
| M3-7 | Statistics: bootstrap over followers (1000 replicates, multinomial counts over per-follower sums), paired comparisons with identical follower draws for both arms, Wilcoxon signed-rank as the second test, Holm correction. | The per-event tables hold sums of squares: the pooled RMSE of a replicate is a weighted sum, so 1000 replicates cost nothing; common random numbers make the paired differences of H5.2 precise. |
| M3-8 | In milestone 3 the transfer protocol P5 runs between the two datasets with trained models (`ngsim`, `follownet_highd`); the full matrix is experiment E4 of milestone 4. | Only two datasets have trained models so far. |

## Milestone 4 (experiments)

The campaigns are defined in `docs/experiments.md`.

| Id | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| M4-1 | Every model is trained on the full training part (milestone 2 used 4,000 events); the main tables use fold 0 of the driver split with seed 0, the seed spread comes from seeds 1 and 2 on fold 0 for all models, and the fold spread from folds 0-4 for the eight-model subset (the clean arms of E2, scored on the probe events). All 24 models on all five folds and three seeds would be 720 runs. | Time on the shared GPU: a full set of 24 models takes 2 to 3 hours per dataset. The driver bootstrap within a fold already gives the uncertainty over test drivers. |
| M4-2 | The eight-model subset of E2 and E4 is `idm`, `gipps`, `mlp`, `lstm`, `mlp_loop`, `lstm_loop`, `ddpg`, `stability_mlp` (persistence next to it at no cost): two mechanistic models, two one-step networks, their closed-loop variants, the policy learner that led the composite on HighD, and the hybrid with the stability penalty. | The specification asks for eight models in E4; the subset spans the families whose behaviour differed most in milestone 3. |
| M4-3 | E2 paired design (R2): for every fold of the driver split the events of a test driver with at least two events are halved at random into leak and probe events; the clean arm is the driver split scored on the probe events, the leaky arm replaces a random subset of the clean training part of the size of the leak set by the leak events; both arms are `Split` objects with explicit partitions (`paired-clean`, `paired-leaky`), five folds, pooled per-event tables, paired bootstrap over drivers with common random numbers, Holm over the models. E2 runs on `ngsim` and `openacc`, the two datasets where followers have several events. | The unpaired comparison of two splits confounds the leak with different test sets (milestone 1 finding on Waymo); the paired design isolates the leak. FollowNet-HighD and Waymo have one event per driver: no leak is possible there. |
| M4-4 | E4 trains the subset on `openacc`, `waymo_hu`, `follownet_ngsim` and `tgsim` (driver split, fold 0, seed 0) and scores every subset run with P1 on the test parts of all six datasets; the matrix reports the relative degradation with driver-bootstrap intervals. | The six datasets with events on this machine; highD, exiD and CitySim need applications. |
| M4-5 | A protocol result is recomputed when the checkpoint it was computed from changed (the provenance hash of the model is compared), and the per-window tables of a transfer evaluation are stored as `p1_<other>.<table>.parquet`. | The main campaign retrains every model of milestone 2; stale protocol files would silently survive. In milestone 3 the transfer evaluation overwrote the own `p1.*.parquet` tables (the JSON summaries and the report were unaffected). |
| M4-6 | `persistence` is scored on the paired arms as well (no training), so that the fold spread of E1 has its reference on the probe events; E2 is reported over all probe events and over the *affected* probe events (drivers with a leak event: 31 % of the NGSIM probe set, 100 % on OpenACC). Bootstrap replicates of the P3 input resample the equilibria only (speeds that converged and track), as the summary does. | The pooled inflation on NGSIM is diluted by the 69 % of probe events whose driver has no leak event. The milestone-3 composite bootstrap resampled every converged speed, which gave persistence a fraction of 0 instead of no value in the rank intervals (the point composite was right). |

## Milestone 5 (leaderboard and report)

The contract is `docs/leaderboard.md`.

| Id | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| M5-1 | An entry is one run directory; it is admitted only when every protocol file carries a complete checklist block whose data version and split agree with the run and whose model hash agrees with `train.json`; refused entries are listed with their problems and never ranked. Event-split entries are listed apart, labelled "driver leak". | The specification: the leaderboard refuses entries without a completed checklist; a protocol file computed from another checkpoint or another data version must not be compared. |
| M5-2 | The main table of a dataset is sorted by the composite traffic-level score (then by the whole-event P1 error); the one-step ratio, the closed-loop errors per horizon, the P2-P4 inputs and the composite rank interval stand next to each other, with the seed spread when seeds exist. Datasets with transfer runs only (OpenACC, Waymo, FollowNet-NGSIM, TGSIM) get a table of P0 and P1 without a composite. | The benchmark's claim is that traffic-level behaviour, not one-step accuracy, should order the models; the other columns let a reader see the disagreement.
